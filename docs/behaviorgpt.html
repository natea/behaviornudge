Hackathon background

Your workday, read as a shopping trip.

BehaviorGPT is Unbox AI's large behavioral model. Given the events in someone's session, it predicts what they'll do next. It was built for e-commerce, where the events are product views and orders. behaviornudge describes your screen time the same way, so BehaviorGPT can answer a new question: you just landed on LinkedIn; is a stretch of scrolling coming?

behaviornudge was built at Sundai Hack 148, a hackathon centered on BehaviorGPT, so this page documents how it was used. It isn't at the core of how nudges work today: rules decide when to nudge, and BehaviorGPT scores those moments in the background. It may be replaced by a temporal point process model, proposed in the EasyTPP drift predictor change.

Four steps

No training on your data: BehaviorGPT is used zero-shot. behaviornudge only translates, asks, and reads the answer.

  1. A catalog of your activities

    Every activity you do often becomes a “product”: linkedin.com/feed, x.com/messaging, an editor on a project.

  2. Sessions as events

    Each session is a view with its real timestamp. A session of 10 minutes or more also counts as an order, a commitment.

  3. Ask what comes next

    Send your last 50 sessions plus the visit that just started, and get the 20 activities it thinks are most likely next.

  4. Turn it into a drift score

    The more of the predicted next activities are consuming (feeds, browsing), the higher the chance of drift.

From a ranking to a drift score

BehaviorGPT answers with a ranked list of likely next activities and a score for each. behaviornudge turns those scores into weights (a softmax) and adds up the share that falls on consuming activities. That share is the probability of drift. The rank of the first consuming item is kept as a second signal.

Where it runs

In the backtest

A rolling weekly evaluation over 28 weeks of history. For every drift-prone visit, each predictor sees only what happened before it. A check in the code raises an error if any later session slips in.

  • BehaviorGPT goes head to head with a baseline built from your own history: past drift rates by site, previous activity, time of day and weekday.
  • Each is scored on PR-AUC and ROC-AUC, and on how its nudges would do within a daily nudge budget.

Live, in shadow mode

Every time the live engine sends a nudge, BehaviorGPT scores that moment in the background, and the score is saved next to the nudge.

  • It never decides or delays a nudge; the rules do that.
  • It gets 3 seconds. If it's slow or fails, the baseline scores the moment instead and the error is logged.
  • All 14 nudges sent at the hackathon got a BehaviorGPT score. Comparing those scores with whether you went back to work is how it earns a say later.

How it did

The backtest covered 781 drift-prone visits over 28 test weeks (April to November 2026). 245 of them turned into drift, a base rate of 31%.

What to make of it

  • The signal is real but small. 0.57 is better than chance, but not yet good enough to decide nudges on its own. That's why it runs in shadow mode.
  • The nudge budget didn't test much. With about 6 drift-prone visits a day, a budget of 5 lets either model nudge most of them. A budget of 1–2 a day would show whether the top-ranked moments are worth acting on.
  • Weekly scores are noisy. Weeks had between 1 and 24 drifts, so one drift can swing a week.
  • Time of day may not matter to it. In one manual probe, shifting a history by 12 hours didn't change the answer.
  • Predicting at the end of a meeting couldn't be judged. Only 3 of 200 meeting ends were followed by drift.

Details that mattered

Real timestamps, nothing recorded

The SDK's convenience call re-stamps history events close to “now”, which would erase when things happened. So behaviornudge builds each request with the SDK's own data models and posts it to the raw /vector/recommendations endpoint itself, keeping each session's real time. It sets register_event: false, so predictions aren't recorded back into the model as new behavior.

What leaves your Mac

Requests go through an allowlisted gate that logs each one locally. A request carries activity keys and timestamps, and the catalog adds readable names, usual mode and domain. Repo names appear in item names. Screen text, page titles, URLs beyond the coarse key, and screenshots are never sent.

Every predictor uses the same small interface: history before a moment, the intent and the current activity in, a drift probability and a ranked list out. BehaviorGPT can be swapped out or compared with others, such as the EasyTPP temporal point process models in the proposed EasyTPP change. Switch predictors in [predictors].
